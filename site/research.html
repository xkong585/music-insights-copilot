01 / Product decision research

Discovery is a moment.
What makes it worth revisiting?

Should a music product invest in more exploration, easier access to familiar music, or a way back to recent discoveries? Start with behavior. Keep intent an open question.

Real listening logs · Historical, observational evidence
Current product decision: investigate the need before building.
Listening logs reveal recorded patterns. They cannot tell us whether a listener forgot an artist, disliked it, wanted variety, or stopped synchronizing.
What should we do next?

Compare opportunities,
not just model scores.

01 · Active explorationUseful if listeners want fresh material. Lower repeat can coexist with a successful discovery experience.
02 · Familiar-content accessUseful if listeners deliberately return to established favorites. More exploration is not automatically better.
03 · Recent-discovery retrievalPrototype only if real episodes show that listeners want to return but cannot easily find the artist.
04 · Improve measurement firstRequired when identity, history coverage or missing intent make a feature recommendation premature.
The next evidence to collect

Ask about the last
real listening episode.

When did you last find an artist you wanted to hear again? How did you try to find it? Did you succeed? When is one listen enough?

These are proposed research questions. No participants have been recruited and no findings from interviews are claimed.

Decision gate

Only move to a narrow retrieval prototype when concrete user episodes support that need. A small task study can test retrieval success and time; it cannot establish long-term retention lift.
